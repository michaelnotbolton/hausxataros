# Cinematic Resolution Principle

The Cinematic Resolution Principle is the MRE's default stance on time within a [[beat|Beat]]: unless timing differences are mechanically encoded in the builds involved, all actions at the same [[time-bands|Time Band]] resolve simultaneously — "when the camera is on your character." A character's 20-second incantation and another character's 5-minute closing argument both land in the same Beat at the same band, because the engine does not simulate elapsed time. It simulates dramatic weight. The table collectively imagines how simultaneous events reconcile. The narrative sorts it out.

---

## The Rule

> **Within a Time Band, unless a character has invested in [[tempo|Tempo]], [[flashstep|Flashstep]], or [[charge|Charge]], all actions are treated as simultaneous.**

Two characters at General band with the same [[momentum|Momentum]] are acting at the same moment. There is no "who goes first" — there is "what does it look like when both of these things happen at once." The Judge and players narrate the reconciliation together.

---

## What Overrides It

The Cinematic Resolution Principle is the *default*. Three investments override it:

- **[[tempo|Tempo]]** — moves a single action to a faster Time Band, establishing clear priority.
- **[[flashstep|Flashstep]]** — creates a cascade of actions across multiple bands, establishing multi-point priority.
- **[[charge|Charge]]** — delays an action to a slower Time Band, accepting that the opponent acts first.

If no one at the table invested in timing, the question "who goes first" does not arise. If someone *did* invest, the investment resolves it. The Principle ensures that timing is always a choice, never an accident.

---

## Why It Matters

The Cinematic Resolution Principle is what makes the MRE feel like a scene rather than a spreadsheet. Two swordsmen crossing blades at the same instant. A lawyer's closing argument and a warrior's charge landing in the same breath. A healer's prayer and a sniper's shot arriving simultaneously. The engine permits all of these and asks the table to imagine how they coexist — because in a system where belief is physics, the drama of the moment matters more than the clock on the wall.

---

## You Died Variant

> **You Died sub-game:** In You Died, the Cinematic Resolution Principle applies identically. Chip-based actions at the same Time Band resolve simultaneously unless Tempo or Flashstep investments create explicit timing separation.
