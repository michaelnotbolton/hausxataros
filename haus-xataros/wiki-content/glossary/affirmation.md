# Affirmation

An Affirmation is the spoken phrase a character declares when activating a [[power|Power]] — a statement of identity compressed into 5 to 17 syllables, where the syllable count becomes bonus d6 dice added to the activation roll. It is not a magic word. It is not an incantation. It is the thing the character *believes*, spoken aloud in the moment they channel it into action. The Affirmation should mean something to the character — not just be metrically convenient. In a world where belief is physics, the words you choose when you commit to an act are part of the act itself.

---

## The Mechanic

> **Affirmation syllable count = bonus d6 dice added to the activation roll.**

A 7-syllable Affirmation adds 7 dice. A 17-syllable Affirmation adds 17 dice. The range is constrained: minimum 5 syllables, maximum 17. Shorter than 5 and the declaration lacks substance. Longer than 17 and it becomes unwieldy in the dramatic rhythm of a [[beat|Beat]].

The bonus dice are added to the Action dice for the Power's roll, contributing [[success|Successes]] toward beating the [[threshold|Threshold]]. This makes the Affirmation a meaningful mechanical component, not flavor text.

---

## Identity, Not Optimization

The Affirmation is declared aloud at the table. It is part of the character's performance — the thing they say every time they use this Power. A character whose Affirmation is "I will not break before I bend" says it every time, in every fight, in front of every audience. The phrase becomes a signature. Over time, it becomes mythology.

The system encourages choosing Affirmations for meaning rather than syllable-count optimization. A 17-syllable Affirmation is mechanically stronger than a 5-syllable one — but a 5-syllable phrase that perfectly captures the character's core is worth more to the fiction than a bloated sentence engineered for dice.

---

## You Died Variant

> **You Died sub-game:** In You Died, Affirmations add bonus chip draws rather than bonus dice, with the same syllable-count-to-bonus conversion. The declaration requirement is identical.
