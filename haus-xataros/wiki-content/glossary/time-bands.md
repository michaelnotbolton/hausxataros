# Time Bands

Not everything in a Beat happens at the same moment — and the MRE gives you levers to control exactly when your intent arrives. Time Bands are the resolution ladder: the ordered sequence of temporal positions within a single Beat. The default is General. Everything else costs something — speed purchased through [[tempo|Tempo]], delay accepted through [[charge|Charge]], or the cascade of multiple arrivals bought through [[flashstep|Flashstep]]. Where you land on the ladder determines whether your action hits before, during, or after your opponent's.

---

## The Ladder

Actions resolve from fastest to slowest. Within each band, resolution follows [[momentum|Momentum]] order (or, more precisely, the result of Momentum allocation after Tempo and Flashstep purchases are resolved).

| Time Band | Position | Who Acts Here |
|:----------|:---------|:-------------|
| **Priority +3** | Fastest | Flashstep Tier 3 cascade actions; Tempo 3 single actions |
| **Priority +2** | ↑ | Flashstep Tier 2 cascade actions; Tempo 2 single actions |
| **Priority +1** | ↑ | Flashstep Tier 1 cascade actions; Tempo 1 single actions |
| **General** | Default | All standard, uncharged actions with no Tempo or Flashstep |
| **ASAP** | ↓ | Charge 1 actions (unless bought off with Tempo) |
| **End of Beat (EoB)** | Slowest | Charge 2 actions; environmental effects; heavily telegraphed abilities |

**Charge 3** actions resolve at the *Immediate band of the next Beat* — a full Beat delay.

---

## How Actions Reach Different Bands

**Upward (faster):**
- **[[tempo|Tempo]]** moves a single action up the ladder. 1 Tempo = Priority +1. 2 Tempo = Priority +2. Each Tempo purchased shifts one band.
- **[[flashstep|Flashstep]]** creates a cascade of actions across multiple bands. Flashstep Tier 2 grants actions at Priority +2, +1, *and* General — three actions in one Beat.

**Downward (slower):**
- **[[charge|Charge]]** delays an action in exchange for [[restriction-bonus|Restriction Bonus]] effects. Charge 1 pushes to ASAP. Charge 2 pushes to End of Beat. The tradeoff is real: your power is stronger, but your opponent has the entire earlier portion of the Beat to disrupt you.
- **Tempo can buy off Charge.** 1 Tempo per band step back toward General and beyond. A Charge 1 power (ASAP) can be pushed to General for 1 Tempo, or to Priority +1 for 2 Tempo. The Charge discount stays; only the timing penalty is removed.

---

## The Cinematic Resolution Principle

Within each Time Band, unless timing differences are mechanically encoded in the builds involved, all actions resolve simultaneously — "when the camera is on your character." The table collectively imagines how simultaneous events reconcile. A character's 20-second incantation and another character's 5-minute closing argument both resolve in a single Beat at the same band unless the player *chose* to build them with Charge restrictions.

The Cinematic Resolution Principle is the MRE's default: simultaneity unless the player has invested in timing. Tempo, Flashstep, and Charge are the investments that override it.

## Resolution Order (Fully Specified)

1. Priority +3 (Momentum order among characters at this band)
2. Priority +2 (Momentum order)
3. Priority +1 (Momentum order)
4. General (Momentum order)
5. ASAP (Momentum order)
6. End of Beat (Momentum order)

Momentum ties within a band are broken by: Mental Stat Rank → Physical Stat Rank → coin flip.

## Strategic Notes

**Tempo vs. Flashstep on the ladder.** Tempo puts one action at a high band. Flashstep puts *multiple* actions at multiple bands, cascading down. A character who buys Tempo 2 acts once at Priority +2. A character who buys Flashstep Tier 2 acts at Priority +2, then +1, then General — three times. Tempo is cheaper per speed tier; Flashstep is more expensive but generates pressure across the entire Beat.

**Charge users are predictable.** A Charge 2 power broadcasts itself. The opponent has Priority +3 through General to respond before it lands. But Charge 2 also grants significant Restriction Bonus, making the eventual hit far more powerful. The timing vulnerability is the price — and Tempo can partially erase it.

## You Died Variant

> **You Died sub-game:** In You Died, Time Bands operate identically. Chip costs replace Momentum dice for Tempo and Flashstep purchases. Card draws can occasionally grant free band shifts (the Quickdraw card provides a Tempo effect; certain Geist trigger cards provide Flashstep tiers).
