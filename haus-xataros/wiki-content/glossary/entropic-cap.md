# Entropic Cap

The Entropic Cap is the cold truth of injury. Your [[dice-hand|Dice Hand]] cannot exceed your current [[pool|Pool]] — belief narrows as the body falters, as the mind empties, as the social self cracks. A character at full health commands their full conviction. A character at half Pool commands half. Death is the Pool reaching zero, the hand going cold, the self unable to roll.

---

## The Rule

**A character's Dice Hand for any Beat cannot exceed their current Pool value in the domain they are rolling.**

Base Dice (from Stat) + Skill Dice + Equipment Dice are calculated normally. But the total hand is then *capped* by the Pool relevant to the action — Physical Pool for Physical actions, Mental Pool for Mental, Social Pool for Social.

If a character has 9 base + skill + equipment dice but their Physical Pool has been reduced to 6, they roll 6 dice when attempting a Physical action. The remaining three dice are unavailable for that Beat in that domain.

---

## What the Entropic Cap Actually Does

**It models desperation mechanically.** Most systems treat damage as subtraction from a hit point total — you lose HP, but your effectiveness stays constant until you die. The Entropic Cap rejects this. In the MRE, taking damage makes you progressively *less capable* of the thing you were damaged in.

A Physical attack that halves your Physical Pool doesn't just move you closer to death. It strips your ability to throw punches, dodge, wield weapons, or resist future Physical pressure at your earlier level. You are less of a physical combatant now, mechanically and narratively at once.

The same applies to Mental and Social damage in their respective domains.

---

## Cascading Effects

Because the [[dice-hand|Dice Hand]] is what gets divided into the three allocation buckets ([[momentum|Momentum]], Defense, Action), a reduced Dice Hand reduces *everything* at once:

- **Less Momentum available** — Flashstep and Tempo purchases become unaffordable or cost-prohibitive
- **Less Defense available** — Threshold drops, making incoming attacks more likely to land
- **Less Action available** — Your attack roll is thinner, even before Affirmation and STAB amplification

An injured character is simultaneously slower, more vulnerable, and less dangerous. The Entropic Cap turns the first wound into pressure toward the second, and the second into pressure toward the third. This is the death spiral the MRE intentionally models.

---

## Strategic Implications

**Speed is a luxury of the living.** Flashstep and high Tempo purchases work best at full Pool. Characters who take early damage have fewer dice to allocate to speed investments.

**Defense becomes more expensive under pressure.** A Defense allocation that felt comfortable at Pool 30 feels risky at Pool 15, because it represents a larger fraction of your shrinking total.

**Healing, Regeneration, and Resurrection matter more than raw damage.** Restoring Pool doesn't just buy survival time — it restores capability. A Regenerate effect that returns 4 Physical Pool effectively returns 4 Physical Dice Hand capacity, which propagates into more options across Momentum, Defense, and Action.

**Pool targeting exploits the Cap.** Attacks aimed at a low-Stat "dump pool" compound the Cap against a character who had that pool small to begin with. Magus's Physical Pool of 12 at Stat 1 meant his Defense Threshold ceiling was 1 regardless of how many Defense dice he allocated (See [[tournament-insights-duel-2]]). The Entropic Cap and Stat-capped Thresholds work together to make glass cannons brittle by design.

---

## You Died Variant

> **You Died sub-game:** In You Died, the Entropic Cap applies to chip pools rather than dice pools. A character's chip allowance per Beat cannot exceed their current damage-tracked chip total in that domain. The design intent is identical — damaged characters have less to spend.

---

## Why This Is Canon

The Entropic Cap is one of the MRE's foundational mechanics and appears in every combat resolution. It has been confirmed across all SRD versions from v3.0 forward. There are no open questions about its core operation.
