# Spontaneous Evolution

Spontaneous Evolution is the mechanic that makes the MRE's combat philosophy concrete: damage funds growth. Every point of damage a character takes in a Beat — from enemy attacks, environmental hazards, even their own [[strain|Strain]] — becomes a d6 roll during the [[cleanup-phase|Cleanup Phase]]. Each 6 produces 1 XP, immediately available for the next [[planning-phase|Planning Phase]]. The character who takes the most punishment gains the most potential. The fighter who is losing is the fighter who is learning.

---

## The Process

During Step 5 of the [[cleanup-phase|Cleanup Phase]]:

1. **Count total damage taken this Beat** — from all sources, including [[strain|Strain]] self-damage.
2. **Roll 1d6 per point of damage.**
3. **Each result of 6 = 1 XP.**
4. **XP is immediately available** during the next Beat's [[planning-phase|Planning Phase]].

The expected return is approximately 1 XP per 6 damage taken (16.7% chance per die). A character who takes 12 damage in a Beat generates an average of 2 XP — enough to construct a 1-[[intensity|Intensity]] [[power|Power]] on the spot.

---

## Mid-Combat Power Construction

The XP from Spontaneous Evolution can be spent on anything XP normally buys: new Powers (2 XP per Intensity), [[stat|Stat]] increases, [[skill|Skill]] ranks. But the signature use is **mid-combat Power construction** — building a brand-new Power during the Planning Phase, tailored specifically to counter the threat that just hurt you.

This is not theoretical. In documented field engagements, a character took 8 points of Physical damage, generated 2 XP, and immediately constructed a 1-Intensity typed Social attack designed to exploit the attacker's low Social [[pool|Pool]]. The attacker entered Social [[gate-state|Gate State]] two Beats later despite having 33 Physical Pool remaining. The underdog mechanic works.

---

## The Tension

Spontaneous Evolution is not an invitation to get hit on purpose. Damage reduces your Pool, which triggers the [[entropic-cap|Entropic Cap]], which limits your [[dice-hand|Dice Hand]], which makes you less effective at everything — attacking, defending, and generating the [[momentum|Momentum]] to act first. The XP gain is a consolation and an opportunity, not a strategy. The character who takes the most damage is genuinely weaker for it. But they have options their uninjured opponent does not.

---

## You Died Variant

> **You Died sub-game:** In You Died, Spontaneous Evolution functions identically — chip loss triggers d6 rolls, with each 6 generating 1 XP for the next Planning Phase. Mid-combat Power construction uses chip-based costs.
