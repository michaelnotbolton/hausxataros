# Power

A Power is a named ability that a character has built from the ground up — not selected from a menu, not unlocked at a level threshold, but *constructed* through an 8-step algorithm that turns concept into mechanics. What you build reflects who you are. The Power called "Final Breath of Suiko" and the Power called "Haymaker" may occupy the same mechanical space, but they are not the same thing — because the character who named one is not the character who named the other. In the MRE, Powers are identity made actionable.

---

## The 8-Step Construction Algorithm

Every Power is built using the same process:

1. **Concept** — What does this Power do, narratively? What does it look like? What does it mean to the character?
2. **[[intensity|Intensity]]** — How much [[cp|CP]] (at creation) or [[xp|XP]] (during play, at 2 XP per Intensity) is invested? Intensity sets the base budget.
3. **[[restriction|Restrictions]]** — What conditions limit when, how, or where the Power can be used? Each Restriction reduces [[strain|Strain]] cost and adds [[restriction-bonus|Restriction Bonus]] Effect Ranks.
4. **[[effect-tag|Effect Tags]]** — What does the Power *do* mechanically? Tags are selected from seven categories (Offensive, Defensive, Control, Enhancement/Debilitation, Utility, Exotic, Meta). Maximum 10 Effect Tags per Power. Restrictions do not count toward this cap.
5. **[[strain|Strain]]** — Calculate the self-damage cost: Intensity minus Restriction discounts, minimum 1d6.
6. **[[affirmation|Affirmation]]** — Write the spoken phrase (5–17 syllables) declared when activating the Power. Syllable count becomes bonus dice on the activation roll.
7. **Strain [[pool|Pool]] designation** — Choose which Pool (Physical, Mental, or Social) absorbs the Strain. This is permanent.
8. **Name** — Name the Power. This is not cosmetic. The name is part of the character's mythology.

---

## The Budget

A Power's total **Effect Ranks** — the currency spent on Effect Tags — equals:

> **Effect Ranks = Intensity + Restriction Bonus**

A 1-Intensity Power with 4 Restrictions has 5 Effect Ranks and costs 1d6 Strain. A 5-Intensity Power with 0 Restrictions has 5 Effect Ranks and costs 5d6 Strain. Same output. Radically different cost. This is the [[creative-reciprocity|1 CP Nuke]] principle: creativity and commitment to identity are rewarded as much as raw investment.

---

## Capacity Ceiling

A single character may possess a maximum of **22 Powers**. Each Power may contain a maximum of **10 Effect Tags** (Restrictions do not count toward this cap). These hard limits prevent combinatorial explosion while allowing enormous build diversity within the constraints.

---

## Keywords and Typing

A Power may carry a [[keyword|Keyword]] — an elemental or domain-specific label (e.g., "Kinetic," "Arc," "Psychic") that activates the [[specificity-modifier|Specificity Modifier]]. Typed offensive effects deal double their rank value (2:1). Typed defensive effects deal 1.5× their rank value. Generic (unkeyworded) Powers operate at 1:1 — weaker output but uncounterable.

Keywords are free. They cost no Effect Ranks and do not count toward the tag limit. The price of typing is not mechanical — it is strategic: your Power now has an elemental identity that can be exploited through the [[hexagon-circuit|Hexagon]] and [[triangle-circuit|Triangle Circuit]] matchup systems.

---

## Power Chassis

Powers fall into broad functional categories based on their Effect Tag composition:

- **Attack** — deals [[damage|Damage]] to a target's Pool
- **Control** — restricts enemy options (Immobilize, Stun, Barrier)
- **Utility** — provides non-combat benefits (Movement, Perception, Healing)
- **Defense** — protects self or allies (Armor, Shield, DR)

A single Power can blend chassis types through its tag selection. A Power with Damage 2 and Perception 1 is both an attack and a scouting tool.

---

## Mid-Combat Construction

Through [[spontaneous-evolution|Spontaneous Evolution]], characters who take damage generate XP that can be spent during the next [[planning-phase|Planning Phase]] to construct entirely new Powers. This is not theoretical — it is one of the MRE's signature moments. The character who just got hit hardest is the one building the counter-weapon, tailored specifically to exploit what they just learned about the enemy.

---

## You Died Variant

> **You Died sub-game:** In You Died, Powers are represented as card constructions rather than dice-based builds. The 8-step algorithm applies, but chip costs replace dice costs and card draws replace roll outcomes. Keywords, Restrictions, and Strain function identically in mechanical logic.
