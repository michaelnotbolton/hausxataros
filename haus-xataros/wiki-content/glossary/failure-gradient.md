# Failure Gradient

Missing is not nothing. In the MRE, the distance between your roll and the [[threshold|Threshold]] measures not just whether you failed but *how badly* — and the universe responds proportionally. A near-miss is a stumble. A catastrophic miss is a disaster that feeds the [[entropy-pool|Entropy Pool]], handing the Judge ammunition to make the world push back harder. The Failure Gradient ensures that rolling poorly is never a blank space in the narrative. Something always happens. The worse you fail, the worse that something is.

---

## The Three Tiers

When an action's [[success|Successes]] fall short of the [[threshold|Threshold]], calculate the negative [[margin|Margin]] (Successes − Threshold) and consult the gradient:

| Tier | Negative Margin | Consequence |
|:-----|:---------------:|:------------|
| **Soft Failure** | −1 to −2 | The action fails with minimal consequence. You swung and missed but aren't off-balance. You argued your point and it didn't land but nobody thinks less of you. The Judge may offer a minor complication or simply move on. |
| **Hard Failure** | −3 to −4 | The action fails and creates a complication. Your weapon overextends, your composure cracks visibly, your argument opens a line of attack the opposition can exploit. The Judge introduces a concrete narrative consequence. |
| **Catastrophic Failure** | −5 or worse | The action fails spectacularly. Something breaks — your weapon, your composure, the floor beneath you. The Judge describes the disaster AND adds dice to the **[[entropy-pool|Entropy Pool]]**. |

A miss at exactly Margin 0 is not a failure — it means you met the Threshold precisely, a bare success with no excess force. Negative Margins begin at −1.

---

## The Entropy Pool

Catastrophic Failures are the primary fuel for the [[entropy-pool|Entropy Pool]] — a Judge-side resource that grows when players fail spectacularly. The Judge spends Entropy Pool dice to introduce complications, empower NPCs, or trigger environmental hazards. Entropy represents the universe's tendency toward disorder, given mechanical teeth.

The Entropy Pool creates a feedback loop: a character in a bad position (low [[pool|Pool]], shrinking [[dice-hand|Dice Hand]] from the [[entropic-cap|Entropic Cap]]) is more likely to roll poorly, more likely to hit Catastrophic Failure thresholds, and thus more likely to feed the Entropy Pool — which makes the situation worse for everyone. The death spiral has a communal dimension. One character's collapse can darken the entire battlefield.

---

## Applying the Gradient

The Failure Gradient applies to all rolls with a Threshold — both [[power|Power]]-based combat actions and [[scene-action|Scene Actions]] (skill checks, environmental interactions, social maneuvers). A Catastrophic Failure on a Scene Action feeds the Entropy Pool the same as a Catastrophic Failure in combat.

The Judge determines the specific narrative consequence within each tier. The system provides the severity; the fiction provides the shape. A Hard Failure on a sword strike might mean the blade lodges in a wall. A Hard Failure on a legal argument might mean you accidentally conceded a point the opposition will exploit next Beat. The mechanical tier is fixed. The story is flexible.

---

## Strategic Notes

**The Failure Gradient punishes overreach.** A character with a small Dice Hand attacking a heavily defended target is more likely to produce a large negative Margin. If your 4 Action dice face a Threshold of 6, even a total whiff puts you at −5 or worse — Catastrophic territory. The gradient incentivizes knowing when *not* to attack. Sometimes the smart play is to allocate everything to Defense and [[momentum|Momentum]], survive the Beat, and wait for a better opening.

**Catastrophic Failure feeds your enemies indirectly.** The Entropy Pool is not targeted — it is a communal pool the Judge uses against the party as a whole. One character's spectacular failure can result in a complication that hits a different character entirely. Team coordination matters even in failure.

## You Died Variant

> **You Died sub-game:** In You Died, the Failure Gradient operates identically on chip-based rolls. Catastrophic Failures feed a card-based Entropy reserve the Dealer draws from to introduce complications. The three-tier structure and Margin thresholds are unchanged.
