# Tempo

Tempo is the moment before the moment. In Jotunnberg, where belief is physics, Tempo is conviction arriving ahead of schedule — the spear-point of will that shapes when your intent lands in the shared imagined space of combat. One Tempo is a single step faster. Two is a band jump. Enough Tempo, and you can strip the [[charge|Charge]] off your most patient powers and let them fly now, before your enemy can react.

---

## What Tempo Is

Tempo is a purchase made from the [[momentum|Momentum]] sub-pool during the [[allocation-phase|Allocation Phase]]. It manipulates *when* a single action resolves within a Beat, without granting additional actions (which is [[flashstep|Flashstep]]'s function).

**Conversion rate:** 3 Momentum dice = 1 Tempo. *(Provisional — subject to playtest tuning.)*

Tempo purchases are declared face-down during Allocation and revealed with the rest of the Beat's commitments. There is currently no cap on Tempo purchases per Beat.

---

## What Tempo Does

### 1 Tempo — Priority Advantage Within a Time Band

Your action resolves at Priority +1 within its current [[time-bands|Time Band]], ahead of any action in the same band that did not purchase Tempo.

**The Cancellation Rule.** If two opponents both spend 1 Tempo for actions in the same Time Band, they resolve at Priority +1 together — simultaneous again. Priority escalation is *relative*, not absolute. Spending the minimum Tempo is a hedge against opponents spending the minimum Tempo, not a guarantee of going first.

This is by design. Tempo rewards commitment over defensive matching. If you actually want to be first, you either spend more than your opponent or you move to a faster Time Band entirely.

### 2+ Tempo — Time Band Escalation

Each additional Tempo beyond the first moves your action up one Time Band on the resolution ladder:

| Time Band | Tempo from General |
|-----------|-------------------|
| End of Beat (EoB) | — |
| ASAP | — |
| **General** | **0 (default)** |
| Priority +1 | 1 Tempo |
| Priority +2 | 2 Tempo |
| Priority +3 | 3 Tempo |

The Priority tiers (+1, +2, +3, etc.) are the same bands that [[flashstep|Flashstep]] purchases occupy. Tempo lets a single action reach them without buying Flashstep — cheaper than the full action cascade, but you get only the one action.

### Tempo as Charge Buyoff

The [[charge|Charge]] Restriction forces a power to resolve in a later Time Band (ASAP for Charge 1, EoB for Charge 2, etc.) in exchange for Restriction Bonus effects. Tempo can be spent to buy off this delay — one Tempo per band step back toward General or beyond.

**Example.** A power with Charge 1 normally resolves in the ASAP band. Spending 1 Tempo brings it to General. Spending 2 Tempo brings it to Priority +1. The Charge discount stays; only the timing penalty is removed.

*(Rate provisional: 1 Tempo per band step. Subject to playtest.)*

---

## Tempo vs. Flashstep — The Speed/Volume Tradeoff

Both mechanics let you act faster. They differ in what else they do.

**Tempo** moves one action. If you spend 2 Tempo, you act once at Priority +2 and that's all.

**Flashstep** multiplies actions. If you buy Flashstep tier 2, you act at Priority +2, Priority +1, *and* General — three actions.

Tempo is cheaper per speed tier; Flashstep is more expensive but scales output. A character who expects to land one decisive attack and wants it to land first buys Tempo. A character who wants to apply sustained pressure across the Beat buys Flashstep. A character who does both runs out of Momentum quickly and has little left for Defense or Action amplification.

---

## Strategic Notes

**Tempo is most valuable against opponents with expensive attacks.** The faster your killshot lands, the less likely you are to trade damage. Against low-commit opponents, the Cancellation Rule means spending just 1 Tempo often yields nothing.

**The bluff economy matters.** Because Momentum allocations are face-down until Reveal, you can hide Tempo commitments in what appears to be a standard Momentum-heavy allocation. Opponents who reveal first are often over-committing to Defense based on uncertainty about what you bought.

**Against Charge-tagged attackers, small Tempo investments pay off.** The Closing Argument / Carnage Shot matchup in the Tournament Insights demonstrated this: 1 Tempo on an Immediate-band attack against an ASAP attacker is usually decisive. (See [[tournament-insights-duel-6]].)

## You Died Variant

> **You Died sub-game:** In You Died, Tempo purchases are made with chips rather than dice. The Cancellation Rule still applies, and chip costs scale with the speed gain — 1 chip to win a Time Band tie, more to jump to the next band. Card draws during play can sometimes grant free Tempo effects (the Quickdraw card explicitly provides this). See [[you-died/tempo-yd]] for the full chip schedule.
