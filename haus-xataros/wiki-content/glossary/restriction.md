# Restriction

A Restriction is a mechanical limitation on how, when, or where a [[power|Power]] can be activated — and it is simultaneously a gift. Every Restriction a character accepts reduces the Power's [[strain|Strain]] cost and grants bonus [[effect-tag|Effect Ranks]] through the [[restriction-bonus|Restriction Bonus]]. Restrictions are not penalties. They are the character's mythology made mechanical: the swordsman who can only strike while standing, the psychic who must speak her intent aloud, the lawyer whose argument requires a physical courtroom. The condition is the character. The condition is also the vulnerability.

---

## What Restrictions Do

Each Restriction applied to a Power provides two benefits:

**Reduced Strain:** The first 4 Restrictions each subtract 1d6 from the Strain roll (minimum 1d6 floor). A 3-Intensity Power with 2 Restrictions costs 1d6 Strain instead of 3d6.

**Bonus Effect Ranks:** The first 4 Restrictions each add +1 Effect Rank. Beyond 4, diminishing returns apply (see [[restriction-bonus|Restriction Bonus]] for the full curve).

---

## Common Restriction Types

Restrictions define conditions that must be met for the Power to activate:

- **Somatic** — requires a specific physical gesture or stance
- **Verbal** — requires a spoken component (beyond the [[affirmation|Affirmation]])
- **Environmental** — requires a specific location, terrain, or condition
- **Positional** — requires a specific facing, distance, or spatial relationship
- **Temporal** — limits when the Power can be used (e.g., only once per Scene)
- **[[charge|Charge]]** — delays the Power to a later [[time-bands|Time Band]]

Each Restriction must be specific and exploitable. "Must be standing" is a Restriction an opponent can exploit by knocking the character prone. "Must be in combat" is not a meaningful Restriction during combat. The Judge has discretion to reject Restrictions that don't create genuine vulnerability.

---

## The Vulnerability Tradeoff

Every Restriction is a condition an informed enemy can deny. A character with four Restrictions on their primary attack has four ways to be shut down — and an opponent who learns all four can render the character's strongest tool unusable. This is [[creative-reciprocity|Creative Reciprocity]] in its purest form: the more powerful the Power (through Restriction Bonus), the more readable and exploitable the character.

Information gathering — learning an opponent's Restrictions — is therefore a strategic action with mechanical consequences. The [[effect-tag|Perception tag]] exists partly for this reason.
